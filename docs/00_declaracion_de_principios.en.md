# Declaration of principles

A set of principles has guided the design of the **GENis** system, from its conception and throughout its entire development cycle. They are:

- **TRANSPARENCY**: "algorithmic transparency" is an essential attribute for the scientific validation of the tool from its logical-mathematical dimension. In its legal dimension, it is a necessary condition for the exercise of the right to due process, which requires public knowledge about how the systems used to produce expert reports operate. Around the world, judicial authorities rely on these systems to hand down rulings that affect people's lives and freedom. Given these values to protect, it is essential to provide the possibility that expert results can be reproduced by the parties through alternative methods and on independent platforms. Foregoing this would mean resigning oneself to a forensic "black box" and facing the risk of numerous challenges. Throughout the GENis development cycle, the recommendations of the DNA Commission of the International Society for Forensic Genetics (ISFG) on transparency, availability, and reproducibility for computer systems that perform biostatistical calculations in forensic applications have been taken into account.

- **COLLABORATION**: the GENis system is the product of the joint effort of multiple stakeholders from different countries, coming from the scientific community, universities, judicial bodies, security forces, private-sector companies, and civil society organizations. This community promotes open and verifiable contribution to the code, documentation, and standards, ensuring traceability, quality control, and continuous improvement of the system. On the other hand, as transnational crime grows in magnitude and mutates in its forms, greater judicial cooperation between countries becomes indispensable. The active promotion of bilateral data-exchange mechanisms, or even the creation of multilateral registries, is inscribed in the system's functionality roadmap, with a detailed instance-interconnection module.

- **SECURITY**: the GENis system applies the concept of "security by design," which means understanding security as a dynamic process that is, therefore, permanently reviewable and improvable. In the system's current versions, this concept is reflected through a set of attributes: i) two-factor authentication; ii) a user and role model; iii) traceability and audit capabilities over actions within the system; iv) the use of cryptography and distributed ledger technologies (blockchain).

[1] The GENis development cycle was guided by the "Digital Public Goods Standard" developed by the Digital Public Goods Alliance (DPGA) and by the "Principles for Digital Development" proposed by the Digital Impact Alliance (DIA).

- **FREE / OPEN SOURCE SOFTWARE**: The GENis system was born as a digital public good and draws on its values and standards to guide its evolution. The publication of its source code puts into practice the principles of transparency, collaboration, and security for all of GENis's direct and indirect users. It is hosted in the Fundación Sadosky's public GitHub repository under the GNU Affero General Public License Version 3 (AGPL v3).

All the tools used to create the GENis system's code are free / open source licensed, as are its dependencies and associated software.

To uphold our principles of transparency, collaboration, security, and free/open source software, we will share all information related to user reports, we will not hide errors, and we will continue publishing GENis under a free software license, without exception.

**Available at:** *https://github.com/fundacion-sadosky/genis*
